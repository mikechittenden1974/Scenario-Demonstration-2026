# StarPost Express Ltd — Scenario Brief

## SD2 Training Pilot | Analyse, Visualise and Interpret

---

## The Organisation

**StarPost Express Ltd** is the galaxy's most reliable parcel delivery company.

Operating from its hub on Orbital Station Meridian, StarPost delivers packages across five galactic sectors — from bustling colony worlds to remote asteroid mining bases, deep-space stations, and ship-to-ship transfers mid-voyage.

StarPost is trusted by millions. Their tagline:

> *"Anywhere in the galaxy. Before your coffee gets cold."*

---

## The Team

Three colleagues are counting on you to make sense of the delivery data.

---

**Director Lyra Korvax** — Operations Director

Lyra keeps the whole operation running. She wants to know which sectors are generating the most revenue and whether the delivery performance data suggests any obvious improvements. She prefers dashboards over spreadsheets, and she always wants a plain-English summary.

> *"I don't need to see every row. I need to see the story."*

---

**Dex Fable** — Senior Data Analyst

Dex loves a Pivot Table. He has already cleaned and treated the delivery dataset for you — checking field types, removing duplicates, and standardising the data. The dataset is ready to analyse. Dex wants you to verify the summaries, spot the patterns, and build the charts.

> *"The data is clean. Now we need to know what it's actually saying."*

---

**Zenn-9** — Head of Courier Logistics (and part-time robot)

Zenn-9 coordinates all five pilot couriers. She (Zenn-9 uses she/her) suspects that delivery distance has a direct effect on the fee charged — and wants to see a scatter chart to test that idea. She also wonders whether certain sectors have better customer ratings than others.

> *"If there's a pattern, I want to see it. If there isn't, I want to know that too."*

---

## Your Role

You are a **Data Technician** working in StarPost's analytics team.

You have been given a **cleaned and treated dataset** of deliveries made between January 2025 and June 2025.

Your job is to:

1. Analyse the dataset and answer the business question.
2. Spot patterns using formulas, filters and conditional formatting.
3. Summarise the data using Pivot Tables and Pivot Charts.
4. Build a clear dashboard that Lyra can use.
5. Investigate the relationship between delivery distance and the fee charged.
6. Write a plain-English interpretation of what the data shows.

---

## The Business Question

> **Which delivery sectors and parcel classes generate the most revenue — and are there patterns in delivery distance, time, and customer ratings that can guide route optimisation?**

---

## The Dataset

The dataset covers **75 delivery records** from January 2025 to June 2025.

It includes:

- Delivery date and sector
- Parcel class and destination type
- Distance travelled (in astronomical units)
- Delivery weight and fee charged (in credits)
- Delivery time (in hours)
- Customer rating (1–5)
- Courier pilot ID
- Delivery status (On Time, Delayed, Returned)

**The data is clean and ready to analyse.**

You do not need to import files. You do not need to merge tables. You do not need to fix headers or data types.

Dex has already done that part. Your job starts here.

---

## What You Will Produce

By the end of this pilot, you will have:

- A set of conditional formulas answering key questions
- A review flag identifying deliveries that need attention
- Filtered views revealing hidden patterns
- Pivot Tables summarising performance by sector and class
- Charts showing trends over time
- A clean dashboard with slicers and a timeline
- A scatter chart with a trendline
- A plain-English interpretation of the data
- A final analysed output file: **02_workbook_data/sd2_complete.xlsx**

## A Note on Tone

This is a training exercise. There are no wrong answers — only clues.

If a formula gives you an unexpected result, that is useful information. If a chart shows a surprising pattern, that is worth noting.

You are not being assessed on whether the data confirms a hypothesis. You are being assessed on how carefully you look at what the data is actually telling you.

> *"A pattern is a clue, not a final answer."*
